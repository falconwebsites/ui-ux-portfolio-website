Investment App · Product Design

Making investing simpler for NRIs.

A mutual fund investment experience designed to help NRIs onboard, complete their investment setup and start investing in India with greater clarity and confidence.

Role UI/UX Designer
Focus Product Design · UX · UI
Domain Fintech · Mutual Funds
Platform Mobile Application
01
The context

Investing in India shouldn't feel complicated from abroad.

For an NRI, investing in Indian mutual funds involves more than simply choosing a fund.

Users may need to provide personal information, tax residency details, professional information, Indian bank details and nominee information before they can start investing.

The challenge was to turn this collection of requirements into an onboarding experience that feels clear rather than administrative.

The design question

How might we make a compliance-heavy investment onboarding experience feel simple, understandable and trustworthy?

02
The challenge

The user isn't just opening an account.

They are making a financial decision while navigating unfamiliar requirements.

01

Too many details upfront

Personal, tax, professional, banking and nominee information can make onboarding feel overwhelming when presented as one large form.

02

Financial information needs trust

Users need to understand why information is being requested before they feel comfortable providing it.

03

NRI requirements add complexity

Tax residency, Indian banking relationships and investment eligibility introduce additional steps compared with a simpler domestic onboarding flow.

04

Users need to know where they are

A long onboarding process needs clear progress, understandable steps and opportunities to review information before submission.

03
Product scope

Start with a clear investment boundary.

The experience focuses on NRIs investing in Indian mutual funds through their Indian NRE or NRO bank account.

Direct funding from an overseas or foreign bank account is outside the scope of this product experience.

INCLUDED

Indian NRE / NRO account

The investment flow supports funding through the user's Indian NRE or NRO banking relationship.

OUT OF SCOPE

Overseas bank funding

Direct investment funding from a foreign or overseas bank account is intentionally excluded from the flow.

04
Onboarding architecture

Breaking one long form into manageable steps.

Instead of asking for everything at once, I structured onboarding as a sequence of smaller tasks.

Each step has a clear purpose, allowing users to understand what they are completing before moving forward.

01

Verify

Email or mobile verification followed by OTP.

02

Secure

Create a 6-digit PIN for convenient account access.

03

Complete

Personal, tax, professional, bank and nominee details.

Onboarding flow — from account verification to investment readiness.
05
Account creation

Keep the first interaction lightweight.

The onboarding experience starts with something familiar: an email address or mobile number.

Users can also continue with Google, reducing friction for people who prefer using an existing identity.

Account creation — email, mobile or Google.
06
Verification & security

Verify once. Then make returning access easy.

OTP verification establishes the user's identity during onboarding, while a 6-digit PIN provides a simpler way to return to the app.

Biometrics can provide an additional convenient access method on supported devices. Higher-risk actions can require stronger verification when necessary.

OTP verification.
Create a 6-digit PIN.
07
Personal details

Ask for personal information when it becomes relevant.

Rather than presenting every compliance requirement immediately, the flow introduces information progressively.

Personal details establish the user's profile before the flow moves into more specialised financial and tax information.

Personal information — introduced as a focused onboarding step.
08
Tax residency

Make compliance information understandable.

Tax information is important, but the interface shouldn't make users feel like they are completing a legal document.

The experience breaks tax residency information into understandable questions and provides context where users may need clarification.

Tax residency — collecting the information required for the investment account.
09
Bank details

Connect the investment account to the right bank.

For the defined product scope, users invest through their Indian NRE or NRO account.

The bank step therefore focuses on collecting and validating the Indian banking relationship needed for investing.

Bank details — setting up the Indian NRE / NRO investment relationship.
10
Nominee details

Make an important requirement feel simple.

Adding a nominee is an important part of setting up an investment account, but it doesn't need to become a complicated form experience.

Users can add a nominee, define the relationship and allocation, add another nominee when needed, and ensure the total allocation reaches 100%.

Nominee details — a structured flow for adding and allocating nominees.
11
Review & declaration

Give users one final moment of confidence.

Before submitting the application, users should be able to review the information they have provided.

A final review step reduces uncertainty and gives users an opportunity to catch incorrect information before completing onboarding.

Review & declaration — confirming account information before completion.
Design principle

Reduce uncertainty before asking users to commit.

12
The dashboard

Onboarding should end with a clear next step.

Completing onboarding shouldn't leave users wondering what happens next.

The dashboard becomes the starting point for the investment journey, showing account status and helping users understand what they can do next.

Dashboard — the starting point after onboarding.
13
Design decisions

Designing for clarity at every step.

Progressive disclosure

Instead of exposing every requirement at once, information is introduced step by step so users can focus on the task immediately in front of them.

Clear progress

Breaking onboarding into meaningful stages helps users understand how much they have completed and what remains.

Explain before asking

Financial and compliance-related questions should provide enough context for users to understand why the information is required.

Review before commitment

A dedicated review stage gives users confidence that their information is correct before completing the account setup.

14
The outcome

A complicated setup became a guided journey.

The final experience turns a large collection of onboarding requirements into a sequence users can understand and complete.

Understand

Users know what information is being requested and why it matters.

Complete

Long forms are broken into focused steps that are easier to work through.

Invest

The completed onboarding leads naturally into the investment experience.

15
What I learned

Good fintech UX is about reducing uncertainty.

Financial products often require users to provide a significant amount of information before they can reach the value of the product.

The designer's role is not to remove those requirements when they are necessary. It is to make the journey through them feel understandable and manageable.

When the process is complex, clarity becomes part of the product itself.
