Futures & Options · Product Design

Making complex options data easier to understand.

An F&O market analysis experience focused on making market data easier to explore and interpret.

Role UI/UX Designer
Focus Product Design · UX · UI
Domain Fintech · F&O
Platform Web Application
F&O DASHBOARD — HERO SCREENSHOT
01
The context

Bringing the trader's workflow onto one screen

The client was an F&O trader who studied options data, noted important values in his book, tracked changes between today and the previous day, and performed calculations to understand market activity.

The idea was to bring this workflow into a single interface, keeping the data, historical context and calculations together on screen.

This would make the process faster and more consistent, reducing manual calculations, the chance of mistakes, and the frustration of keeping track of everything in a notebook.

The design question

How might we turn all of this data into a clear view of the market?

02
The challenge

Making relationships within the data easier to see

The interface brought together a large amount of options data across strikes, CE/PE activity, Open Interest, price movement and OI changes. The challenge was presenting all of this without making the information difficult to scan and interpret.

The useful context often came from the relationship between values — how CE and PE activity compared, how OI moved with price, and where significant changes were happening. With so many values changing across multiple strikes, users needed to quickly see these relationships without having to mentally connect data from different parts of the interface.

03
Understanding the data

Before designing the screens, I had to get familiar with F&O

I started with the basics — understanding options, strike prices, Calls and Puts, Open Interest, and how price and OI changes relate to each other.

I then worked through the relationships between price and OI changes to understand the different market indicators — Long Buildup, Short Buildup, Long Unwinding and Short Covering. I also looked at how market strength could be derived from these movements.

This helped me understand what the data was showing and how traders use it to study market activity, giving me the foundation to design the experience.

04
Information architecture

I structured the experience to make the data easier to explore.

The experience was built around a continuous feed of live market data received from Sharekhan through a socket connection. I organised this data into layers so the most important information appeared first, while the underlying options-chain data remained available for deeper investigation.

05
Reducing the noise

Making dense information easier to scan.

One of the design challenges was helping users quickly identify the strikes where activity was concentrated.

Instead of forcing users to scan the entire chain, I introduced a focused view of the highest-interest strikes.

The Top 6 view surfaces the strongest current Open Interest concentrations, while the extended options chain remains available for deeper analysis.

Related values were grouped into a single cell, using hierarchy and spacing to distinguish the primary value from supporting information. This allowed more information to be visible without spreading related data across separate columns or areas.

06
Strength summary

From raw activity to a readable signal.

One of the biggest opportunities was reducing the cognitive effort required to interpret multiple indicators together.

The Strength Summary brings the different pieces of activity together into a visual comparison between the Call and Put sides.

Instead of asking the user to compare several numbers manually, the interface provides a quick visual indication of where the combined activity is stronger.

Design principle

Simplify interpretation without hiding the underlying data.

07
The outcome

Making complex market data easier to explore and interpret.

The final design doesn't try to predict the market or hide the complexity of F&O data. Instead, it gives that complexity structure, helping users move from a broad view of market activity to the details behind it.

What I would measure

If I were extending the project into post-launch UX evaluation, I would measure whether the redesigned experience made analysis faster, clearer and less effortful, looking at analysis time, comparison time, manual effort, user confidence and time to complete the task.

12
What I learned

Good fintech UX isn't about showing less data.

It is about helping people understand the data they are already looking at.

Working on the F&O product pushed me to think beyond visual design. I had to understand how different market indicators relate to each other and then translate those relationships into a visual hierarchy.

The biggest takeaway was simple:

Complex information doesn't always need to be simplified. It needs to be structured in a way that makes it easier to navigate and understand.
